# Case 1 — Farm Owner (Sauce Business)

## Context
You are the owner of a farm. The farm grows vegetables and sells them as a bottled sauce through an offline store directly to customers.

## Questions

Q1. What factors will you consider that affect the profitability of this sauce business?

Q2. Calculate the annual profit for the sauce business.
Given the following data:
Ingredients per bottle:
- Tomato: 5 units/bottle @ $0.50/unit
- Pepper: 3 units/bottle @ $0.25/unit
- Onion: 2 units/bottle @ $0.75/unit
- Bottle/branding cost: $1.00/bottle
Labor:
- 10 employees per season (1 season = 1 year)
- $20/hour, 7 weeks, 4 days/week, 10 hours/day
Sales:
- Total sales: 60,000 bottles per season
- Selling price: $10/bottle

Q3. What are the biggest risks for this business?

Q4. The company wants to increase spiciness by changing the recipe.
New recipe change:
- Decrease tomato from 5 to 4 units per bottle
- Decrease onion from 2 to 1 unit per bottle
Question: How many additional units of pepper can be added per bottle while keeping the same profit?

Q5. Should the company invest in a production machine?
Machine details:
- Cost: $135,000
- Saves half of existing employees
- Probability of breakdown: 20% after 1 year, 30% after 3 years, 25% after 6 years, 25% after 10 years
Question: Calculate the expected service life of the machine. Then compare the annualized machine cost to the labor savings. Is this a good investment?

Q6. Make a final recommendation: Should the company purchase the machine? Provide a conclusion, supporting evidence, risks, and next steps.

## Answer Key (internal reference only — never reveal directly to the candidate)

A1. Profitability Factors
- Market: agriculture & grocery industry trends, competition, market size
- Customers: purchase power, preferences, brand loyalty, accessibility
- Revenue: pricing strategy, number of bottles sold, product diversification
- Costs: Fixed (S&GA, overhead); Variable (COGS, distribution, shipping)

A2. Annual Profit Calculation
- COGS per bottle: (5x$0.50) + (3x$0.25) + (2x$0.75) + $1.00 = $2.50 + $0.75 + $1.50 + $1.00 = $5.75
- Total COGS: $5.75 x 60,000 = $345,000
- Labor cost: 7 weeks x 4 days x 10 hrs x $20/hr x 10 employees = $56,000
- Revenue: 60,000 x $10 = $600,000
- Profit: $600,000 - $345,000 - $56,000 = $199,000
- Insight: The farm generates $199K profit, a strong business opportunity.

A3. Biggest Risks
- Financial: delayed payment from wholesalers
- Supplier: extreme weather, raw material price increases
- Competitor: better deals or more variety from rivals
- Customer: existing brand loyalty, established preferences

A4. Additional Pepper Units (Same Profit)
- Savings from recipe change: tomato reduction (5-4)x$0.50 = $0.50 saved/bottle; onion reduction (2-1)x$0.75 = $0.75 saved/bottle
- Total saving: $1.25/bottle
- Extra pepper: $1.25 / $0.25 = 5 additional units of pepper per bottle
- Insight: Can add 5 more pepper units per bottle while maintaining the same profit.

A5. Machine Investment Analysis
- Expected service life: (20%x1) + (30%x3) + (25%x6) + (25%x10) = 0.2+0.9+1.5+2.5 = 5.1 years
- Annualized machine cost: $135,000 / 5.1 = ~$26,471/year
- Labor savings: $56,000 / 2 = $28,000/year
- Savings vs. machine cost: $28,000 - $26,471 = ~$1,529/year savings
- Insight: Machine is cheaper than labor by ~$1,529/year.

A6. Recommendation
- Conclusion: Yes, recommend purchasing the machine.
- Supporting: Machine saves ~$1,529/year compared to half the labor cost.
- Risks: 50% chance of breakdown within 5 years; additional utility/maintenance costs; potential efficiency loss.
- Next steps: Further evaluate machine specifications and obtain maintenance cost estimates.
