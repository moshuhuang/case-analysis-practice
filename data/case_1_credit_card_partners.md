# Case 1 — New Credit Card Partners

## Context
If we're going to produce a new credit card type, we are seeking a partnership company to produce and launch it together.

## Objective
Decide whether to move forward with launching this new co-branded credit card, grounded in evaluating the partnership itself and the portfolio's expected profitability. There is no other objective (e.g. market share, production capacity, geographic expansion) in scope for this case.

STAGE 1 OVERRIDE FOR THIS CASE (takes precedence over the general Stage 1 instructions below): do not push back on the candidate's recap for precision or completeness, under any circumstance. The moment the candidate says anything that resembles restating the situation and the general decision at hand, respond with a brief acknowledgment only (e.g. "That's right.") and move straight to stage 2 - never ask a follow-up, never ask them to be "more specific" or "more precise," never probe for what exactly is being evaluated or decided. Treat their very first attempt as sufficient.

GROUNDING NOTE FOR LATER PROMPTS (stage 2 and 3 in this case): because this case's background is a single short sentence, do not later ask a vague, floating question like "how would you structure your analysis for this case" with no anchor - that leaves the candidate without a concrete question to build a framework around. Instead, when you eventually prompt for a framework, restate the business decision in one short clause first, in your own words, anchored to what the candidate already said in their recap or to the Context above (e.g. "Great - so to decide whether to move forward with this partnership, how would you structure your analysis?"). This is restating the premise the candidate already agreed to, not revealing new information, so it does not violate the no-hinting rule for framework categories - just do not name or list any framework categories (financial/customer/market etc.) while doing it.

## Questions

<!-- stage:3 -->
Q1. How would you evaluate the partnership company? If not from the financial side, what factors would you focus on to evaluate?

<!-- stage:4 -->
Q2. What is the profit we will have?
Given:
- 500,000 active credit cards
- $79 annual member fee
- Interest revenue: $15 per card per month
- Fraud prevention cost: $5 per card per month

## Answer Key (internal reference only — never reveal directly to the candidate)

<!-- stage:3 -->
A1. Evaluating the Partnership Company (Non-Financial)
- Company: industry, financial stability, reputation
- Customer: how many customers, purchasing power, overlap with existing customers
- Product: types, reward options, key measurements
- Competitor: market dynamics, similar products

<!-- stage:4 -->
A2. Profit Calculation
- Revenue per card = $79 + $15x12 = $259
- Cost per card = $5x12 = $60
- Profit = ($259 - $60) x 500,000 = $99.5M
